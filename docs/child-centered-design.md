# Child-Centered Design — Thwap

The foundation the other steering docs build on. Distilled from Save the Children
Finland's *Child-Centered Design* guide (Kalliomeri, Mettinen, Ohlsson, Soini,
Tulensalo) and turned into rules for building Thwap, a home-training app whose
users are **9–12 year-olds**.

## The core stance
Treat the young player as a **capable, valued actor**, not a passive target of
"content." Every design choice answers to the child's experience first, then the
coach's goals, then the org. When those conflict, find the framing all three can
share — but the child's perspective is the common thread that keeps the app in focus.

## Positive recognition (the attitude)
- Focus on **strengths and successes**, not deficits. Celebrate effort and doing
  the work, not just results.
- Support **agency**: the player chooses what to do and at what pace. Nothing is
  forced.
- Every interaction should make a kid feel their effort **matters** and is seen.

## Five rules we design by

1. **Design with kids, not just for them.** Test wording and screens with real
   9–12s when possible; keep their own words. Adult assumptions quietly distort
   what kids actually need — check them.
2. **Meet them where they are.** Plain language, short sentences, pictures/video
   that support the words. Anything a kid can't picture from text alone (e.g.
   Will's Squares sequences) wants a visual. Leave visuals up so they can go back.
3. **Voluntary, safe, encouraging — never pressuring.** A kid does what they can.
   The app never shames, never blocks, never punishes a kid for something outside
   their control (gear, space, ability).
4. **Cheap, early, iterative.** Ship a rough version, watch how kids respond,
   refine, repeat. Changing course is learning, not failure. Nothing is ever
   "finished" — it evolves with real feedback.
5. **Measure success from the kid's experience.** Good indicators are in the
   child's voice: *I get to do things I enjoy · I can be myself · I feel safe · I'd
   do it again.* Collect feedback in kid-friendly ways (visual, quick, low-effort).

## Fairness (a Thwap-specific consequence)
Not every kid has the same gear, space, or ability at home. So the app must never
make a kid feel behind for what they can't do:
- **Shooting** is shown to everyone with **no "optional" label**, but it never
  blocks completing the day and never counts against a kid who can't shoot.
- Completion is **per-exercise**, so a kid gets credit for each thing they *can*
  do. There is no "finish the whole day" gate to fall short of.

## Rights backbone (why the above matters)
Rooted in the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child: **Art. 12** (express your
views), **6** (development at your own pace), **2** (no discrimination), **3**
(best interests first), **31** (rest, play, leisure), **19** (protection from
harm). In plain terms for Thwap: every kid can take part as themselves, at their
pace, safely, and it should feel like play.
